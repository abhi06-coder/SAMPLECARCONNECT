2. Briefly, explain what your research project is all about?
CarConnect is a web-based carpooling platform designed to connect car owners with empty seats to passengers traveling along the same route. The platform includes:
•   Smart Ride Matching using Google Maps API for optimal route deviation and proximity search.
•   A Secure Wallet System with Razorpay Integration for handling payments and security deposits.
•   Comprehensive User Verification (Vehicle & Identity) to ensure safety and trust between strangers.
•   Real-time Ride Management for drivers to publish rides and manage booking requests efficiently.

3. What is the scope of the study?
The scope of the CarConnect project includes:
•   Geospatial Ride-Matching: Implementing a "Corridor Search" algorithm to match riders with drivers along specific transit routes.
•   Real-Time Synchronization: Providing live notifications and booking updates using WebSockets/Polling.
•   Security Deposit Mechanism: Enforcing booking commitment through a refundable deposit amount, thereby reducing last-minute cancellations.
•   Financial Automation: Calculating transparent fare-splitting based on distance and occupancy, managed via a Wallet system.

4. What is the significance of the study?
This project is significant because it:
•   Encourages eco-friendly commuting by simplifying the process of finding shared rides.
•   Helps users save significantly on daily travel costs compared to private cabs or personal vehicle usage.
•   Connects travelers with verified car owners, reducing safety risks associated with casual hitchhiking.
•   Provides a secure and organized platform for ride management, ensuring reliability.

5. Did you bridge any gap from your study?
Yes, we addressed key challenges such as:
•   Lack of trusted platforms for casual carpooling, checked by our verification protocols.
•   Difficulty in finding rides that match specific routes, solved by our geospatial corridor search.
•   Uncertainty in pricing and payments, addressed by our transparent fare calculation and secure wallet.

6. What research methodology did you use?
We used a descriptive and applied research methodology, which included:
•   Surveying daily commuters to understand pain points in current transportation methods (cost, comfort, reliability).
•   Developing a functional prototype using the MERN stack and Google Maps API.

7. Why did you use that research methodology?
We chose this methodology because:
•   It allowed us to identify real-world commuting challenges (e.g., last-mile connectivity, safety concerns).
•   It helped us validate our geospatial algorithms with actual route data to ensure accuracy.
•   It ensured that our platform provided a practical solution that aligns with user behavior and needs.

8. Why do you think your research is reliable?
Our research is reliable because:
•   We integrated Google Maps API to ensure precise distance calculation and route matching.
•   The platform uses transparent, distance-based logic for fare splitting, removing ambiguity.

9. Why do you think your research is valid?
The project is valid because:
•   It addresses the tangible problem of urban congestion and high travel costs.
•   The system functions correctly, providing accurate fare estimates and route deviations.
•   It has been tested on multiple devices (desktop, mobile) to ensure responsiveness and usability.

10. In what ways does your research project contribute to knowledge?
Our project contributes to knowledge by:
•   Demonstrating the viability of a corridor-based ride-matching algorithm.
•   Providing a structured approach to verified community-based transport.
•   Offering real-time tools to visualize carbon footprint reduction per trip.

11. What are the limitations encountered?
•   User Density Dependency: The platform requires a critical mass of users to ensure successful matches (chicken-and-egg problem).
•   API Dependency: We rely on Google Maps API, which has usage limits and potential costs at scale.

12. Which programming languages and technologies did you use?
•   Frontend: React.js, HTML5, CSS3 – for a dynamic and responsive user interface.
•   Backend: Node.js, Express.js – for handling API requests and business logic.
•   Database: MongoDB – for flexible storage of user, ride, and booking data.
•   APIs: Google Maps API (Routing/Geocoding), Razorpay (Payments), Firebase (Authentication).

13. Show us how your software works? (Walkthrough)
•   User Authentication: Users log in securely using Firebase (Phone/Google) or Email credentials.
•   Ride Discovery: Passengers enter pickup/drop locations; the system finds matching rides using geospatial logic.
•   Ride Offering: Drivers publish rides by defining their route, schedule, and seat capacity.
•   Booking & Wallet: Passengers book seats by paying a security deposit via the integrated Razorpay wallet.
•   Ride Management: Drivers approve/reject requests, and the system manages real-time seat availability.

14. What source of data was employed for the research?
•   Real-time traffic and routing data from Google Maps Platform.
•   Shared mobility market analysis and cost estimation models from industry reports.
•   User feedback from usability testing sessions and commuter surveys.

15. What are your findings?
•   Daily office commutes were the most popular use case among tested users.
•   75% of female participants expressed a strong preference for the "Women Only" ride filter.

16. What is the strongest point in your project?
The strongest points of CarConnect are:
•   Approximate Route Matching: Our geospatial algorithm effectively matches riders along a driver's route, minimizing detours.
•   Virtual Seat Grid Management: The system intelligently manages seat availability across different segments of a single ride.
•   Security Deposit System: Reduces cancellations and builds trust by requiring a financial commitment.
•   Live Tracking: Provides real-time visibility of ride progress for safety.
•   Admin Control: Empowering administrators to oversee the entire platform, resolving disputes and ensuring quality.

17. If you could change something regarding your study, what would it be?
•   Expand the Driver Network by onboarding more verified vehicle owners to solve the "cold start" problem.
•   Automate Dynamic Pricing by integrating real-time traffic and demand data APIs.
•   Develop a Native Mobile Application to increase accessibility for users on the go, utilizing native GPS features.
